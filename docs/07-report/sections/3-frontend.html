3. Frontend design

3.0 Overview

There are two Flutter desktop apps for Windows. Mooney's (clients/customer-app) is for customers. Mooney's Manager (clients/management-app) is for store staff. Both are built on one shared package, clients/bookstore-ui, which holds the theme, the log-in form, the bill view, the API clients and the common widgets. So both apps look and behave the same.

PointWhat the apps do
ColorsWarm neutrals, plus one accent color (focus rings, selection, links) and one danger color (errors, late rentals). Book covers and the Mooney's sign carry the rest of the color. All values live in one file, bookstore-ui/lib/src/tokens.dart.
Light and darkTwo palettes with the same structure. One button in the top bar switches between them. In the customer app the choice is also a saved preference.
TypeInter for interface text, Source Serif 4 for headings. Both are bundled with the app (SIL Open Font License).
ContrastText colors were chosen to meet WCAG AA (at least 4.5 : 1) in both modes; see tokens.dart. No test measures the ratios.
Color-blind usersNo state is told by color alone. Errors, payment status, availability and ratings always have an icon and a word, or a shape.
KeyboardTab moves between controls, Enter activates, Esc goes back or closes, Ctrl+F jumps to the search field. Focus is shown with a 2 px outline.
Screen readersEvery icon-only button has a tooltip, which is also its name for screen readers.
Responsiveness (N2)All calls to the services are asynchronous. Buttons show a spinner while a request runs, and are disabled so nothing is sent twice.

The screenshots below are drawn by Flutter from the apps' real widgets, with the real fonts and sample data, at 1440 x 900 (clients/customer-app/test/screenshots_test.dart and the manager app's equivalent). They are not photos of the running window. That the apps work against the real services is shown in the user acceptance test videos (see the video links) and recorded in docs/06-testing/requirements-test.md.

Before any screen was built, each app was drawn as a wireframe in draw.io (milestone M2). The wireframes fixed the core screens of the PRD and what is on each. Later milestones changed some of them and added more screens; the table after the figures lists the changes. Each window of the two wireframe sheets (docs/02-design/diagrams/wireframes-customer-app.png and wireframes-manager-app.png) is shown on its own, so its labels can be read on paper.

3.2 Customer app: Mooney's

3.2.1 Log in

  • The form is the shared log-in form from bookstore-ui; the manager app uses the same one.
  • The password field has a show/hide button.
  • "Create an account" is right under the main button, for new customers.

3.2.2 Create an account, with errors

  • The app checks the fields before sending: username at least 3 characters, a valid-looking email, password at least 8 characters, and the two passwords the same (account_rules.dart).
  • Each problem is shown under its own field, with an icon and a sentence, not by a red border alone.
  • Problems are shown only after the first attempt, so the form does not scold the user while they type.

3.2.3 Search: cover grid

  • Search as you type: the app waits 250 ms after the last key, then asks book-service (GET /api/books?keyword=...). There is no Search button.
  • The shelves (All, Fiction, Nonfiction, Kids, Science, Technology) filter by category. The chosen one is bold and underlined, not only colored.
  • The heading always says what is shown, for example "81 books".
  • The top bar holds grid/list switch, light/dark, "Your order" with its item count, the account button and log out.

3.2.4 Search: list view

  • The grid is for browsing; the list is for scanning. Both show the same results.
  • The heading says how many books match: "3 of 81 books match in title or author".
  • The customer can make the list the default view in the account preferences.

3.2.5 Book detail, adding to the order

  • The detail panel slides in from the right and the grid makes room for it, so the results stay visible.
  • Both prices are on the buttons: "Buy $9.99" and "Rent for 7 days $1.00". The customer never has to work out the 10% rent price.
  • A book already in the order says so ("In your order") and cannot be added twice.
  • A toast confirms the action and offers Undo.

3.2.6 Book detail with reviews

  • Stars are drawn in the ink color, filled, half filled or outlined, so the rating is read by shape, and the number is written next to them ("4.0 · 3 reviews").
  • "Your review" comes first. Its Edit and Delete buttons are under the review text, below the bottom edge of this figure. Other reviews follow, newest first.
  • A customer writes one review per book, through PUT /api/books/{id}/reviews/mine.

3.2.7 Your order

  • Each row has a Buy / Rent switch with both prices, and a rented book shows its due date ("Due back 25 November 2026 if ordered today").
  • The summary on the right counts bought and rented books and shows the total before the order is placed.
  • "Place order" is the one main button. The line under it says the bill will be shown and emailed.

3.2.8 The bill

  • The bill on screen has the same parts, in the same order, as the HTML bill that is saved and emailed.
  • The line at the top says where the copy was sent.
  • Payment status is a chip with an icon and a word ("Payment pending"), not a color.
  • "Pay now" leads to the pretend checkout (3.2.10).

3.2.9 Your account

  • Preferences (dark mode, open search as a list) are saved on this computer, per account, in %APPDATA%\CSCE310 Bookstore\preferences.json.
  • A late rental is told by a warning icon and the words "Was due back 2 days ago", not by color alone.
  • Paid and pending orders have the same row height, so the list reads as one column. Each order opens its bill.

3.2.10 Pay dialog

  • It says plainly that it is a demonstration and charges nothing.
  • No card details are asked for or stored.
  • One main button, with the amount on it ("Pay $58.00").

3.2.11 Dark mode

  • Dark mode uses a warm near-black and off-white, not pure black and white, and its text colors were also chosen to meet WCAG AA.
  • Order 1045 now shows the "Paid" chip with a check mark; order 1044 still has "Payment pending" and a Pay button.
  • The switch between modes is animated (380 ms).

3.3 Manager app: Mooney's Manager

3.3.1 Log in

  • It is the same shared log-in form as the customer app, asked to accept only the role MANAGER.
  • A customer who tries to log in is turned away with "This app is for managers. Customers log in to the customer app."
  • There is no "Create an account": managers are added by the store.

3.3.2 Orders

  • The heading says how many orders wait for payment. The filter (All, Pending, Paid) shows a count on each choice.
  • "Mark as paid" sends PATCH /api/orders/{id}/payment-status. The toast offers Undo, which sets the order back to pending.
  • Paid and Pending are a filled and an outlined chip, each with an icon and a word.
  • The three tabs (Orders, Books, Reports) sit in the top bar.

3.3.3 An order's bill

  • The bill is drawn by the same shared BillView widget the customer sees, so both sides see the same bill.
  • "Print bill" gets the saved HTML bill from order-service (GET /api/orders/{id}/bill), saves it to a file and opens it with Windows (normally in the browser), from where the manager prints it to post to the customer (FR9).

3.3.4 Books

  • The same search as the customer app: as you type, by title or author, with Ctrl+F.
  • Low stock is written out with an icon ("Only 2 left"), not shown by color.
  • "Add book" is the one main button of the screen.

3.3.5 Book form, with validation

  • The form checks each field before saving (book_rules.dart), and the problem appears under its field ("Enter the price"), with an icon.
  • The shelf is chosen from five buttons, so it cannot be misspelled.
  • The cover image address is optional. Without it, the app draws a cover from the title.
  • The line at the top says changes reach the customers' search at once.

3.3.6 Reports

  • Period buttons at the top: Today, Last 7 days, Last 30 days, This month, All time. The chosen one is filled and has a check.
  • Four tiles give orders, revenue, books sold and books rented, each with a line of detail.
  • Sales by day are bars in the ink color, with the day under each bar and the amount over it, so the chart can be read from its words.
  • Best sellers are a plain numbered table, most copies first.
